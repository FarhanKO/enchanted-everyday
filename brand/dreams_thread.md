# Fann's lost dreams: the season plan

The long thread from the story bible, as ordered steps. The story architect, script writer and critic all get this
file. Code works out which steps have been told and offers only the next one (`engine/canon.py`). This file is the
owner's to edit. Each step id appears as a `### Dn.` heading, in telling order. Rename a step's title freely, but
never renumber a step that has been told.

## Where the pilot left it (s000)

Fann sold his dreams at the Midnight Shop, one night at a time, to pay what he owed. He kept only the oldest one:
a boy, a giant tree on a cliff, a sea so blue it hurt. His sold dreams are out in the city, being dreamed by
strangers. The girl who couldn't sleep, who was dreaming them and smiling, is Tina. That night someone left a
letter beneath the tree in Fann's dream: "Thank you. Keep this one."

What the season is building toward, never said aloud: the letter is Tina's. She wrote it without knowing who
she was thanking, and never sent it. The Impossible Post Office delivered it anyway.

## How a step is told

- **One step per thread episode, in order.** A card that moves the thread names its step in `dreams_step`.
- **A step is a direction, not a script.** Invent the details: the place, the object, the stranger's name. Keep it
  to one moment, and keep the step small inside a story that is about something else.
- **It must work for a first-time viewer.** Someone who has never seen the pilot sees a complete story with a
  strange, lovely detail. Only regulars see the step.
- **What a step invents becomes canon.** The canon note records it, and later steps reuse it. Steps marked
  *reuses* depend on an earlier step's detail: find it in the dreams-thread section or the story so far.
- **Never:** say that Tina is the dreamer, let Fann and Tina meet or speak to each other before D10, suggest
  romance, or give Fann his dreams back. Those are the owner's decisions for a later season.

## Steps

### D1. Three falling notes

In an ordinary moment (waiting for a kettle, a bus, a lift), Tina hums three falling notes and can't say where she
learned them. Regulars know them as Fann's hum. No one in the story remarks on it.

### D2. A place she has never been

Tina wakes from a dream of one specific, ordinary place from someone else's childhood: a kitchen with a painted
door, a rooftop with a kite, a stairwell that smells of rain. She remembers one concrete detail of it, and it
stays with her all day. The episode invents the place. The canon note must record it exactly.

### D3. Where something used to be

*Reuses D2's place.* Fann passes something that should mean something to him (the same detail Tina dreamed) and
feels nothing where a feeling used to be. The cost of selling, made small and concrete. He doesn't know why it
hurts.

### D4. Not only her

A stranger elsewhere in the city (invented, named, with their own story) dreams a fragment of one of Fann's
dreams: the mustard scarf on a small boy, or the sea so blue it hurt. The dreams are spreading. Tina does not
appear.

### D5. The price of buying back

Fann returns to the Midnight Shop between 12:00 and 12:07 and asks for one dream back. The Shopkeeper names a
price that isn't money, and the episode never says exactly what it is. He doesn't pay. Not yet.

### D6. The tree in a frame

Tina sees the giant tree from the cliff in waking life: a faded postcard, a painting in a café, a photograph in a
stranger's wallet. She recognises it from her dreams, though she has never seen the sea. She keeps it, or can't
stop looking at it.

### D7. A letter she never sends

Tina writes a short thank-you to whoever's dreams she has been having: "to whoever dreams of the sea". She knows it
can't be sent and puts it away. Show her handwriting and the word "Thank you", never the whole letter.

### D8. The Impossible Post Office

*Reuses D7's letter.* At the Impossible Post Office, an unsent letter in Tina's handwriting is sorted. Its address
is not a street but a place: beneath a tree on a cliff above the sea. Never say whose letter it is or link it to
the pilot out loud.

### D9. Almost

Fann and Tina are in the same place on the same night, a minute apart, each facing their own decision. The Last
Cafe suits it. His three notes and her dupatta's music-box answer sound together for a moment. Neither looks up at
the right time.

### D10. They meet

After a long time of almost, Fann and Tina are in the same place and this time one of them looks up. They meet over
something small and ordinary: a dropped glove, the last dry seat, an umbrella that only covers one. She hums the
three falling notes without thinking, and he knows them. Neither says why that matters. The episode ends with them
still talking, not with a promise. Its lead is both of them. From here on they know each other, and about two
episodes in five are theirs together.

## After D10

The thread rests. No card moves it until the owner adds the next steps here. Whether romance follows, whether she
learns she was dreaming his dreams, and whether he ever gets them back, is the owner's call.
